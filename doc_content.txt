Chapter I
Project Context
         Rice is one of the most essential food grains in the world. It provides the principal source of food for more than half of the worldâ€™s population and plays an important role in food security, as well as being a vital economic crop, particularly in developing countries in Southeast Asia. For many farmers, rice cultivation is the primary source of income. Nevertheless, rice production is limited by plant diseases, which reduce yields and involve significant costs if not diagnosed and treated promptly.

         Today, most farmers identify rice diseases using only visual assessment, which is often time-consuming and relies on subjective criteria. This approach can be associated with considerable delays in the decision-making process since the disease may no longer be treatable when visible symptoms appear. Moreover, many farmers do not have convenient access to qualified phytosanitary inspectors who can quickly find an appropriate solution to the problem.

         Agricultural extension services currently provide inadequate and untimely advice to farmers, leaving them to deal with disease management on their own. An urgent need has therefore arisen for an intelligent system capable of providing rapid and reliable diagnosis of rice diseases and recommending a subsequent course of action. Such a system would make it possible to find optimal solutions to emerging problems and minimize losses.
     
        To address this issue, this study will develop OryzaTix, an image processing-based system utilizing the MobileNet model to recognize and classify common crop diseases and to identify its severity level. The system then recommends the appropriate course of management according to the identified problem. The developed prototype includes accessibility features such as voice-to-text technology, an embedded AI chatbot, and voice-enabled AI consultation in both Tagalog and English to aid local farmers in using the system.

Objectives
General Objective
This study aims to design, develop, and evaluate OryzaTix, a cross-platform application that detects rice crop diseases using the MobileNet model while recommending management practices.
Specific Objectives
To design and develop a cross-platform application with an intuitive graphical user interface and back-end system for detecting rice diseases.
To train and integrate the MobileNet model for detecting and classifying rice crop diseases based on images of infected leaves and recommend their severity levels.
To develop features that can be accessed and utilized by farmers with diverse literacy levels, such as voice-to-text command, AI chatbot, and voice-to-voice AI consultation integrated with Tagalog and English language models.
To develop a module that recommends management practices depending on the detected rice disease and its corresponding severity level.
To evaluate the designed system based on disease detection accuracy, response time, and level of acceptance from the target users.

Scope and Limitations
Scope
This study was conducted to develop Oryzatix: An Image Processing-Based System for Rice Crop Disease Detection using MobileNet with Management Recommendations that can help users detect their rice crop diseases using image processing and provide management recommendations.
In this study, the following are included:
Detecting selected rice crop diseases using image processing and MobileNet;
Classifying the level of severity of the detected disease based on the infected rice leaf;
Providing management recommendations depending on the detected rice crop disease and its level of severity;
Designing the system with an AI-based chatbot feature to help the user ask about rice diseases and crop management;
Allowing the user to use voice or text;
Providing translations for the users who prefer to use Tagalog or English;
Allowing the user to capture an image using his/her camera or upload an image;
Displaying disease history tracker for users to monitor the diseases, severity level, detection date, and management recommendations of the rice crop;
Designing the system for Rice Farmers, agricultural experts, students, researchers, and anyone interested to identify and manage rice crop diseases;
Developing the system into a cross-platform application so that it can be accessed by multiple platforms using different applications;
Evaluating the efficacy of disease detection and the overall performance of the system such as its performance, ease of use, reliability, and user satisfaction.
Limitations
This study involves the use of the following limitations:
The system can only detect rice crop diseases that were included in the database, and it cannot recognize any other diseases;
The system can only detect rice crop diseases in leaves and not in stems, roots, panicles, grains, etc.;
A higher accuracy on disease detection and severity classification is expected for high-quality images captured or uploaded in the system;
AI-powered chatbot can only provide information and answer queries about rice crop diseases, severity levels, and management recommendations included in the system and should not be used to ask unrelated questions or specific inquiries that require an answer beyond the systemsâ€™ database;
The systemâ€™s voice-to-text feature is only available for Tagalog, English, and other languages are not supported. Speech-to-speech translation and voice recognition accuracy may differ depending on the userâ€™s voice, accent, articulation, and background noise;
The system is designed as a cross-platform application, and its performance varies from one device to another;
Detection of rice diseases and management recommendations provided are only for reference and should not be used for other purposes such as crop scouting, weather prediction, forecasting, and estimation;
The accuracy of MobileNetâ€™s detection and classification of rice crop diseases and severity levels are dependent on the quality of data used to train the model, which may vary due to differences in rice varieties and environments;
The system requires an internet connection to utilize the AI capabilities and provide responses using the chatbot, voice-to-text, voice-to-voice, and translation features, which is unavailable when not connected to the internet.

AI chatbot response
Disease history record

Bracino et al. (2023) proposed a vision-based deep learning system for the non-destructive classification of healthy and diseased paddy rice leaves. The researchers evaluated EfficientNet-B0, MobileNetV2, and Places365-GoogLeNet in identifying several bacterial and fungal diseases, including Bacterial Leaf Blight, Bacterial Leaf Streak, Bacterial Panicle Blight, Downy Mildew, Hispa, and Rice Tungro Disease. Among the evaluated models, EfficientNet-B0 achieved the highest average classification accuracy of 97.74%. The study emphasized that deep learning provides a fast and accurate approach for disease diagnosis without damaging the crop. This study is related to OryzaTix because both systems use deep learning-based image classification to identify rice diseases automatically and assist farmers in making timely disease management decisions.

(2025) proposed a Rice Plant Disease Detection, Classification, and Mapping System Using Drone that combines YOLOv9 Tiny, drone imagery, and a mobile application to detect Bacterial Leaf Blight, Brown Spot, and Rice Blast in Philippine rice fields. The developed model achieved 98.2% validation accuracy and 87.8% testing accuracy based on expert evaluation from the Department of Agriculture. The system also integrates georeferenced mapping to identify the exact location of infected crops, improving disease monitoring and field management. This study is related to OryzaTix because both systems employ artificial intelligence for automated rice disease detection, although OryzaTix focuses on image-based diagnosis through a web and mobile platform rather than drone-assisted monitoring.

Mirandilla et al. (2023) conducted a study on the use of spectral analysis for detecting and differentiating three major rice diseases in the Philippines, namely tungro, bacterial leaf blight (BLB), and rice blast. The researchers analyzed the spectral reflectance of infected rice leaves using visible and near-infrared wavelengths (400â€“850 nm) to determine the unique spectral characteristics of each disease. The study found that the red and red-edge wavelength ranges were the most sensitive in distinguishing the diseases, while regression models such as Partial Least Squares Regression (PLSR), Random Forest Regression (RFR), and Support Vector Regression (SVR) showed promising results in estimating disease severity. The findings demonstrated that spectral analysis can support early disease detection and improve disease monitoring and management practices. Synthesis 
         The reviewed literature reveals that the trend is towards developing methods for rice disease diagnosis based on image analysis rather than the laborious manual inspection. Local researchers utilized deep learning, convolutional neural networks, spectral analysis, and digital image processing techniques to identify rice diseases, estimate the percentage of infection, and predict the most appropriate interventions.

         Foreign researchers also report that convolutional neural networks, transfer learning, image segmentation, and comparison of models can be applied to classify rice diseases. Moreover, the accuracy of rice leaf blight detection is found to be dependent on the quality of data, preprocessing, class distribution, environmental conditions during image capture, and the modelâ€™s ability to generalize beyond the training set.

         The MobileNet and MobileNetV2 are essential to OryzaTix because they ensure the balance between classification accuracy, processing speed, and model complexity. It is critical for web and mobile apps for farmers who use devices with lower performance. Besides, multiple articles mention the need for disease-severity estimation. They describe systems that can quantify infected regions or infection severity; however, there is an essential distinction between disease recognition and severity assessment, and management and treatment recommendation systems.

         In general, most solutions described in the articles address only a few aspects of crop disease management. For example, the systems allow for disease classification, field monitoring with computer vision, mapping, severity estimation, and treatment recommendation. There is a lack of lightweight MobileNet diagnosis, severity classification, management recommendation, Tagalog-English bot, voice assistant, disease history, and multilingual application for farmers. Thus, Oryzaixâ€™s application is critical for addressing the detected gap.

         The evaluation should prioritize crop image analysis, classification accuracy, performance, and usability testing and response time. It is vital to promote OryzaTix as a decision-support system rather than a substitute for agricultural experts or diagnostic laboratories.

System Analysis and Design
System Overview
         Oryzatix is an image processing-based system that was designed to help detect and manage selected rice crop diseases through the use of the MobileNet deep learning model. Through the system, users can upload or take images of infected leaves for the detection process. The detected disease and its corresponding severity level are then presented to the user. Based on the results of detection, the application recommends ways to manage the disease to aid the user in his/her disease control efforts.

         Moreover, the system features an AI-powered chatbot that can be accessed through voice and text. The chatbot offers assistance in two languages, namely, Tagalog and English. Users can ask the chatbot questions or request assistance using voice or text. The system also has a feature called the Disease History Tracker which lets users keep track of detected diseases that they had previously uploaded or inputted into the system.

        Because it is a cross-platform system, Oryzatix can be used on different devices so long as they have internet connectivity. The chatbot, voice recognition, and response 
generation features require internet access in order to function. By combining image processing functions and artificial intelligence, Oryzatix aims to be a convenient system for rice farmers, students, researchers, farm workers, and other individuals concerned with rice cultivation, particularly with detecting and managing rice crop diseases.
